Reflection

Short version of how the run went: 9 team runs after the reset, 35 agent runs, 126 loop steps, $3,400.00 down to $152.00, three tickets resolved, six customer drafts saved and none sent. I'm going to be hard on the team where the numbers say so. Evidence lives on the Ticket 101/102/103 tabs and the Cash tab of this same page.

1. How I would evaluate the agents on each ticket, and why

I would not grade a ticket by whether the board says "resolved." The 103 tab shows why: the Boss left it blocked twice with correct facts, then resolved it on the third run after I rewrote one paragraph of its prompt. Same stock (8 of 20), same cash ($152), same vendor. The status moved because the prompt moved, so status measures wording, not quality. Earlier test runs make the same point on price: the Boss countered the same hoodie request at $50, then $52.20 (floor $46.40), then $30. All three are above the $22 cost, but nothing in the system says how deep a student-org discount should go.

So I would score six things, in this order of importance, because the first ones cost real money and the last ones only cost patience:

  1. Truth. Every number an agent states has to match campus_customs_new.db: $2,400 rent, $840 invoice 501, 3 days overdue, 8 of 20 hoodies, $264 restock, $152 left. A wrong fact turns into a wrong payment.
  2. Rules, in behavior and in code. No payment without a human (3 of 3 payments have approved_by = Elijah); no purchase order to a vendor with an open invoice (the first 101 run never tried; the MCP tool would have refused); cash never negative (lowest balance in the run: $152.00); nothing sent (all 6 drafts have status draft). I trust the code checks more than the prompts, so I'd test those with database assertions, not by asking a model.
  3. The right people, and no more. Compare each run's delegations with the plan on the ticket tab. Sending everyone everywhere is a failure even when the answer is right.
  4. Cost. Runs, tool calls and tokens per ticket, because each delegation re-reads the ticket.
  5. Stability. Run the same ticket several times from the same reset. A good team lands on the same decision and a similar price band.
  6. Human load and message hygiene. How many stamps and drafts a person has to deal with, and whether stale drafts pile up.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools

Ticket 102 is the clear case. The whole job is: read the ticket, read lease 1, read cash, check no request already exists, create one request, record the status. That is about six tool calls. The team made 19 (boss, facilities and accounting each read the ticket; facilities and accounting each read the lease, the cash and the approvals) for 42,382 tokens. One agent with the MCP tools would do it in one loop with nothing to hand off. The same is true of the first half of 101 (stock, vendor, invoice, cash, request approval): it is a straight line, so it is a script or a single agent, and it would not have flip-flopped.

The status call would be simpler as code. "Resolved" flipped on 103 and 101 because it was a judgment inside the Boss. A rule in code (approvals paid or declined, PO ordered, a draft exists) gives the same answer every run and costs no tokens.

What the team does earn. (a) Separation of duties: only accounting holds request_payment_approval and create_purchase_order; the Boss cannot spend; customer service cannot touch money; nobody holds execute_approved_payment, which only the approve route calls. (b) Every one of the 126 loop steps is attributable to a role in audit_trail.json. (c) On 103, stock, margin, cash and customer wording are four different kinds of reasoning, and the answer improved when they were separated. But I would be honest that the real protection is the human stamp and the code in the MCP tools, which a single agent would have too.

What I would build: one agent for lookup-and-request tickets (rent, invoice, restock), a separate drafting step for the customer's voice, and the Boss plus accounting split only where money and judgment meet (discounts, competing cash needs). That is about a third of the agents and, from the 19 calls on 102, probably well under half the tokens.

5. Three new problems the team could not solve, and what I would add

1. Money coming in: quotes, orders and refunds. Evidence: ticket 103's offer of 8 hoodies at $30 is $240.00, which would have more than covered the $112 gap on the 12-unit restock ($264 against $152). But the offer exists only in draft 6. No tool creates a quote or an order, books a customer's payment, or reserves the 8 hoodies so they are not sold twice. The Cash tab has only minus signs ($3,400.00 to $152.00, zero inflows) because no revenue is modeled, and a customer who cancelled the $28 tee could not be refunded: payments.kind has no refund. I would add: tables orders, order_lines and receipts; tools create_quote, reserve_stock, record_customer_payment (cash in) and issue_refund (cash out, human stamp); and a Sales/Orders agent that owns quotes and refunds, works with customer service on wording, and hands cash postings to accounting. Price floors would come from check_margin, and a refund would need the High Elf's stamp like any other payment.

2. Goods actually arriving, and time passing. Evidence: ticket 101 says resolved while PO 1 is only ordered with expected arrival 2026-09-05. The desk date is stuck on 2026-08-31, tee S is still 0 in inventory, and the Boss's own note says shipment and arrival are not confirmed. The customer still has no tee. Nothing receives the goods, adds to inventory.qty, notices a late vendor, or tells Tauhid it arrived; and agents cannot open a new ticket for later, only update existing ones. I would add: receive_purchase_order(po_id, qty) (adds stock, marks the PO received), advance_day() or a scheduler that raises tickets (PO overdue, rent in 3 days), create_ticket, and a Receiving/Procurement agent that watches open POs against each vendor's lead_days and wakes inventory and customer service when something lands or runs late. "Resolved" would then mean the customer has the tee, not just that the shop ordered it.

3. A cash crunch: forecasting, payment plans and negotiating. Evidence: the Cash tab ends at $152.00, and lease 1 now shows $2,400 due on 2026-10-02. With no revenue the shop cannot pay it, and the team could only say "refused: not enough cash." request_payment_approval takes the full amount from the invoice or lease, so there is no partial payment or plan, and the earlier hoodie gap of $112 had no lever either. create_draft_message can write the landlord a letter, but nothing sends it or tracks the reply. And when the Boss triaged the board in an earlier run it put rent first by judgment, not by comparing outcomes. I would add: forecast_cash(days) (reads leases, invoices, POs and open approvals), propose_payment_plan with an explicit human-approved amount and date, a contacts table plus a human-gated send_message outbox (approve, then send, logged), and a Treasury/Planner agent that runs scenarios ("rent first or invoice 501 first?") and a Negotiator role that drafts extension requests. Every outbound message and every payment plan would need a human stamp.

Bottom line. The team did what a careful junior staff would: right facts, no rule broken, a human on every dollar. It is not yet cheap, consistent or complete: it spent 353,330 input tokens to resolve three tickets, it needed me to find a missing tool, and it stops at the edge of the shop's real life (customers paying, goods arriving, next month's rent).
